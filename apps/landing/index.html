07 · Quick-Start FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is OpenAxe free and open source?

Yes. OpenAxe is licensed under GPLv3 and completely free to use across the Web Studio, native Desktop app, and VST3/CLAP/AU DAW plugins — with no subscriptions, accounts, or telemetry.

What is Neural Amp Modeler (NAM) and where do I get captures?

Neural Amp Modeler captures the exact nonlinear tone of physical guitar and bass amplifiers, pedals, and studio gear as lightweight .nam files. You can drop your own local .nam and .wav IR files onto any Amp or Cab block, or sign in to Tone3000 inside OpenAxe to browse community captures.

Do I need to install anything to play right away?

No. Open openaxe.pages.dev in any modern desktop or mobile browser to run the same C++20 DSP engine inside an AudioWorklet via WebAssembly — including built-in guitar DI loops if you don’t have an audio interface plugged in yet.

How do I open the unsigned macOS Desktop app on first launch?

Beta macOS disk images are not Apple-notarized yet. After dragging OpenAxe to Applications, right-click (or Control-click) the app icon, choose Open, and confirm in the Gatekeeper prompt. Subsequent launches open normally.

Where do I install the VST3, CLAP, and AU plugin bundles?

On macOS, copy .vst3, .clap, and .component bundles into ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/{VST3,CLAP,Components}. On Windows, copy bundles to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3 (or CLAP). On Linux, extract them to ~/.vst3 or ~/.clap, then rescan in your DAW.

Are my presets and tone library uploaded anywhere?

Never. Everything stays local-first: the Web Studio stores rigs and cached captures in your browser’s IndexedDB, while Desktop and DAW plugins share a single local application data directory on your computer.
